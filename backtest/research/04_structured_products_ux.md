# Research 4 - Structured-product, insurance-pool and UX precedents

*Compiled 26 Sep 2026 by a research agent (~83 fetches). Evidence flagged where thin.*

## 0. Framing in numbers

- **The premium budget from carry is tiny.** At ~4% T-bill yield, one week of carry is ~7.7 bps of the yield-leg notional. A 2-trading-day ATM put on a 30%-vol stock costs roughly 1.1% of notional. Carry funds ~7% of an ATM put. "Principal protection" from carry alone is only affordable as a **deep-OTM disaster buffer** or with a **partial participation rate**, exactly as PPNs and buffer ETFs do. (Our simulation agrees: a 20% T-bill sleeve fully funds cover on index ETFs and about a fifth of the cost on Nvidia.)
- **Weekend gaps in tokenized stocks are real but modest on average.** Labor Day weekend 2026 saw $1.01bn of tokenized-stock volume with US markets closed (bStocks $303.5m, xStocks $87.1m, Ondo $43.8m); on Ondo weekends were only 0.55% of volume through May 2026; off-hours spreads 19-33x wider; weekend token moves "anticipated the following Monday open instead of overshooting it". The hazard is *Monday-open jump risk on the reference asset* plus *weekend liquidity/oracle risk*, not chronic weekend drift.

## 1. Table of mechanisms

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Principal-protected notes** | ~85% of principal buys a zero-coupon bond; remainder buys options. At 5y tenor, 20% hard protection ~ 85% participation | Embedded markup; SEC 2012 sweep forced "issuer estimated value" disclosure | Works: record $149.4bn US issuance 2024. Criticism: complexity correlates with higher headline rates and lower ex-post returns (Celerier & Vallee, QJE 2017) | Direct template: yield leg = zero; Keeper pool = option writer. | **Autocallables / Korean ELS** | Investor sells a knock-in put for a fat coupon | Coupons 5-50% | HSCEI-linked ELS: KRW 4.6tn losses on 10.4tn principal H1 2024; StanChart $100m compensation | Anti-pattern for the holder; it *is* the Keeper payoff. Barrier depth and correlation are the killers. |
| **Buffer / defined-outcome ETFs** | Deep ITM call + put spread (buffer) + short call (cap), 1-year outcome period; cap floats with rates and vol | FT Vest 0.88%, Calamos 0.69%; Innovator $27.5bn | $70bn (Jul 2025) to $87bn (Nov 2025), 400+ funds; 100% "max buffers" ~$3.5bn but fastest-growing. Offer a ladder of depths; expect demand spikes into known-risk weekends; enforce "enter before Friday close, settle Monday". |
| **CPPI** | Exposure = m x (V - Floor); m 4-5; gap risk when the risky asset falls >1/m between rebalances; 1987 unwinds | Buy-high-sell-low drag, "cash-lock" | Issuers write a gap put on the structure; dynamic multipliers mitigate | Intra-weekend rebalancing is impossible (reference closed), so the epoch is pure OBPI. ## 2. Design rules extracted

1. **Fund protection from the zero, not from selling a barrier.** PPNs and 100%-buffer ETFs scaled; Cega and Korean ELS blew up. Any "protected" label must survive a -50% Monday.
2. **Accept that 4% carry buys a disaster buffer, not an ATM put.** Offer tiers like buffer ETFs and let the depth float with rates and vol.
3. **Price with an external benchmark plus utilisation, never with the deposit ratio alone.** Y2K's ratio pricing collapsed; Ribbon's lumpy auction leaked vol points. Use Nexus's curve anchored to a realised-vol model (our v4), and split the Friday auction.
4. **Size junior capital to the correlated worst case, not the average.** On a macro Monday every tokenized stock gaps together: MCR must assume correlation ~1 across names within an epoch. (Our bootstrap: March 2020 = 9.2% of covered notional over two weekends.)
5. **Junior/senior attach points explicit and renewed each epoch.** Centrifuge's 15-30% TIN buffer and Tranchess's rebalance band are legible; reset NAVs every Monday.
6. **Fixed-rate tranche needs a visible outside benchmark.** Tranchess tied BISHOP to Venus's USDC borrow rate. Peg senior Keeper yield to USDY/T-bill + spread.
7. **Do not depend on emissions for either side.** Model the pool at zero incentives.
8. **Settle parametrically, on a defined oracle, with a null-epoch refund.** Define "Monday open" as a specific print (first non-null underlying print at or after nextOpenTime) and publish it.
9. **Epoch integrity is the product.** Buffer ETF outcomes only hold for holders present start to end. Lock deposits from Friday close and forbid mid-epoch changes.
10. **Underpricing frequency is the recurring killer.** Sherlock's 3% implied one event in 33 years; reality was ~10x. Backtest against every Monday gap since 2005 (done: results/20_weekend_panel) and require premium >= realised gap loss + margin over a rolling window.
11. **Randomise or distribute the sale.** Split the Keeper auction across Thursday-Friday windows or use RFQ.
12. **Keep a reassurance layer above junior.** Neptune's reassurance fund covered <=25% of pool loss; Sherlock's Nexus excess above a deductible. A protocol-owned backstop at 10-25% of junior makes the senior tranche saleable to treasuries.
13. **Publish an "issuer estimated value" per epoch.** Show holders: carry earned, premium paid, expected payout, fee.
14. **CPPI across epochs, OBPI within.** The weekend leg is a bought put; across weeks, accumulated carry is the cushion and protection depth scales with it.
15. **Expect demand to be event-driven.** Cboe recorded 2x demand elasticity for deeper buffers in April 2025. Capacity and pricing must surge around earnings and FOMC weekends or the pool is adversely selected.

## 3. UX patterns: gamified-not-gambling

**What regulators and app stores flag (avoid):**
- Massachusetts' $7.5m Robinhood order (Jan 2024) banned confetti, scratch-off free-stock reveals, "tap up to 1,000 times/day" waitlist mechanics, popularity lists and emoji push notifications. The SEC's 2021 DEP request singled out "games, streaks, contests with prizes; points, badges, leaderboards; celebrations for trading". (https://www.velaw.com/insights/game-over-robinhood-pays-7-5-million-to-resolve-gamification-securities-violations/ ; https://www.yalelawjournal.org/forum/on-confetti-regulation-the-wrong-way-to-regulate-gamified-investing)
- Yotta: 0.2% APY plus a lottery ticket per $25; 85,000 customers, $112m frozen after Synapse; California fined it $1m (May 2026) for "you can't lose your money"/FDIC claims. Never claim safety you do not custody, and never make the *reward* the lottery.

**Patterns that increase adoption of protective products (use):**
1. **Round-up / spare-change protection** (Acorns: 14m+ users, $33bn invested, avg $800 rounded up; Revolut: 1m vaults): auto-divert a slice of weekly carry to a deeper buffer.
2. **Protection streaks, not trading streaks** (Duolingo: streaks ~2x daily retention): "42 weekends protected" is a loss-aversion hook regulators have not objected to when it rewards *not* acting.
3. **Giveback / mutual framing** (Lemonade: fixed fee, leftovers to charities, $2.1m/yr; 3-second payouts): unused Keeper premium visibly returned removes the "house wins" feel.
4. **No-loss prize framing only if principal is genuinely intact** (PoolTogether): a "Monday bonus" drawn from *surplus* carry is defensible; drawn from principal is Yotta.
5. **Rebate framing over deductible framing.** Johnson, Hershey, Meszaros & Kunreuther (1993): identical policies framed as rebates were preferred to deductibles; Sydnor (2010): households paid ~$100 to cut an expected $20 loss. Show "Monday cash-back if the stock gaps" rather than "loss above 5% is covered." (https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fapp.2.4.177 ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01065313)
6. **Peace-of-mind receipts.** Losses weigh ~2x gains and small probabilities are overweighted. A Monday morning "your floor held / paid out" notification is an order confirmation, not a call to action.
7. **Outcome-period disclosure like buffer ETFs**: show remaining buffer, cap and time-to-settlement at all times.
8. **Sober defaults**: one-tap "protect every weekend" subscription, no leaderboard, no red/green pulse.

## 4. Institutional angle (evidence thin)

- Protocols overwhelmingly **self-insure**: Ethena's reserve ($62m, ~9x adviser-estimated need), Venus per-pool risk funds, Aave Umbrella. They pay **fixed fees for risk management** ($1.6-5m/yr to Gauntlet/Chaos) rather than premiums.
- The only clean protocol-to-protocol cover purchase found is **Sherlock buying 25% excess cover from Nexus above a $500k deductible**.
- Would a lender pay weekly to cap weekend liquidation losses on bStocks? No precedent. The pitch that matches observed behaviour is *reinsurance above a deductible on a named collateral bucket*, priced below the LTV haircut the protocol would otherwise impose; Venus's THE episode ($2.15m bad debt despite 8,048 liquidation txs) is the internal argument that liquidation alone does not clear gap risk.

## 5. Five quotable facts

1. US structured-note issuance hit a **record $149.4bn in 2024, up 46%**, while defined-outcome ETFs reached **$87bn by Nov 2025** with 100%-protection funds the fastest-growing slice.
2. Korean HSCEI-linked autocallables lost **KRW 4.6tn on 10.4tn of principal** in H1 2024.
3. InsurAce collected about **$94,000 of premium** against **$11.7m of UST claims**; Sherlock's pool fell **from $30m to $3m** after pricing a 3%/yr premium that implied one exploit every 33 years.
4. Ribbon's weekly auctions cleared **1.7-2.8 vol points below Deribit**, and IV dropped up to **15 points** before synchronised DOV sales in January 2022.
5. Tokenized stocks traded **$1.01bn over Labor Day weekend 2026** with markets closed, yet weekends were **0.55%** of Ondo volume and off-hours spreads **19-33x wider** than daytime.
